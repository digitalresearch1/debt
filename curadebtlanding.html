Do You Qualify?

Debt settlement may be an option for those with certain financial situations

You May Be Considered If:

  • You have $20,000+ in unsecured debt
  • You're struggling with minimum payments
  • Credit cards, medical bills, or personal loans
  • You want to explore alternatives to bankruptcy
  • You have a steady income source

Debt Settlement May Not Be Right If:

  • Your debt is primarily student loans or secured debt
  • You have pending lawsuits from creditors
  • You need debt relief in less than 12 months
  • You're current on all payments and can afford them

Frequently Asked Questions

Information about debt settlement and how it may work

How does debt settlement affect my credit score?

Debt settlement may negatively impact your credit score. The extent of the impact varies based on your individual credit history and other factors.

What types of debt qualify?

Unsecured debts including credit cards, medical bills, personal loans, and collection accounts may be considered. Secured debts and student loans generally do not qualify.

Is debt settlement better than bankruptcy?

Debt settlement and bankruptcy are different options with different consequences. A qualified professional can help you understand which path may be appropriate for your situation.

How much does the program cost?

Fees are typically 15-25% of the enrolled debt amount, but you only pay when a debt is successfully settled. No upfront fees. Fee structure explained during consultation.

Can creditors still contact me during the program?

While we handle creditor communications on your behalf, some creditors may continue to contact you. Results vary by creditor.

How long does the process typically take?

Some clients may see their first debt settled within 4-6 months. The full program may take 24-48 months depending on your situation. Results vary.

Is the forgiven debt taxable?

Debt forgiven may be considered taxable income. Consult with a tax professional regarding your specific situation.

Can I still use my credit cards?

No — you must stop using enrolled credit cards once you enter the program. Continuing to use them may affect your eligibility.

Important Considerations

Debt settlement programs may negatively impact your credit score and may result in collection efforts. Not all creditors will agree to settle, and results vary by individual situation. Clients are generally expected to stop making payments directly to creditors while in the program, which may lead to increased interest, fees, and collection calls. Please speak with a qualified professional to determine if this program is right for your financial situation.
